01

Financial Services · Multi-State

A sales operating system, built end to end.

  • Lead
  • Grow
  • Run

The challenge. A financial services firm that funds construction and service contractors was expanding into new states. Its new sales hires needed a way to find the right contractors and open a credible conversation in markets where nobody knew the firm yet.

The approach. As fractional Director of Strategic Operations, I segmented the market by trade, metro, contract history and certification. Prospect lists came from public contract-award data, which shows who has been winning work and at what size. The playbook and CRM were designed around those segments afterward.

Delivered. A master playbook: quick-start onboarding and a daily dashboard; scenario call scripts and objection handlers; a pain-point matrix by trade; follow-up templates and weekly scorecards; a seasonal campaign calendar; a referral engine. Alongside it came the CRM pipeline design and territory route planning, plus a referral-partner collateral series written separately for bankers, accountants, primes, subcontractors and community referrers.

02

Construction Materials · Southwest

A feasibility study that ends in a verdict in plain words.

  • Launch
  • Fund

The challenge. Founders planning a construction-materials venture needed to know whether it would work, and needed a document a lender could read and reach the same answer from.

The approach. I built a monthly financial model and extended it to ten years, with a base case, a stress case and sensitivity on price. Beside it sat a failure-mode-and-effects table covering 24 ways the venture could go wrong. The capital structure came last, with decision gates that release money only when a stated condition has been met.

Delivered. The report and model with a recommended capital structure, ending in a one-line verdict of the “feasible as a business, not feasible as currently financed” kind. With it went a diligence list and 59 follow-up questions, ranked by how far each answer would move the model.

03

Small Business · Workforce Development

Workforce programs mapped onto a hiring plan.

  • Fund
  • Run

The challenge. A growing company had a hiring plan, little cash to carry new staff through training, and no clear view of which public programs it qualified for.

The approach. I worked directly with the regional workforce board to map On-the-Job Training reimbursement and Work-Based Learning programs onto the hiring plan, role by role. For each, that meant pinning down eligibility and the board’s documentation and monitoring rules.

Delivered. A funding pathway for the priority hires: job descriptions written to program requirements, candidate paperwork in the form the board uses, and a training-plan structure tied to the owner’s growth plan.
