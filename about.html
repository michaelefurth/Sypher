The Firm & The Principal

The person you meet does the work.

Sypher Solutions gives owners of growing businesses the kind of senior, cross-functional attention large firms save for large clients, from someone who then stays and does the work.

A Note from the Principal

“I started Sypher because I kept meeting capable owners who were stuck between advice they couldn’t implement and vendors who couldn’t see the whole business.”

Most of my work happens after the strategy deck. As fractional Director of Strategic Operations for a multi-state firm that funds contractors, I built the sales operating system end to end: segments by trade and metro, prospect lists from public contract-award data, a playbook with call scripts and weekly scorecards, and collateral for the bankers and accountants who send referrals.

I’ve written feasibility studies, including one for a construction-materials venture in the Southwest. It rested on a ten-year monthly model and a table of 24 failure modes, and the conclusion fit in one plain sentence. I’ve also worked with a regional workforce board to fit On-the-Job Training reimbursement to a client’s hiring plan.

My background is in biomedical device and formulation research, where a result counts only if it can be reproduced. I still plan R&D for inventors; submissions go through qualified regulatory counsel. Forensic bookkeeping uses the same habit: follow each exception to its source. Sypher is not a CPA or law firm, so findings go to your CPA or attorney.

More of my time now goes to automating intake and scheduling for small teams. When you work with Sypher, you work with me. The client list stays small so nothing waits in a queue.

The Name

Sypher takes its name from the cipher: a message that holds its meaning until someone takes the time to decode it.

Most businesses have their own version: competing priorities and half-connected systems that hide what to do next. The work is reading it carefully enough to find an answer you can act on, and then doing it. The ribbon in the mark is the same idea drawn out: many strands, one continuous path.
