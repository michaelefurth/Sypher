The Sypher Method

The same four steps, every time.

A website and a research roadmap go through the same four steps, so you always know which one you’re in and what comes next.

  1. Unravel

    01

    Unravel

    Interviews first, then a close read of the numbers and the systems, to see how work actually moves through the business today.

  2. Decode

    02

    Decode

    We find the binding constraint, which is often not the one that hurts most, and cut the issues down to a short list of priorities.

  3. Uncover

    03

    Uncover

    A roadmap in which every item has an owner and a due date. Then a budget, and the few measures that will show whether it’s working.

  4. Solve

    04

    Solve

    We build it with your team. When it’s done you get the documentation and the logins, and you don’t need us to keep it running.

Questions

Questions, answered plainly.

What does “boutique” mean here?

It means one principal. Michael Furth scopes every engagement and does the work himself, from the first call to the handover. The client list is kept small so your work doesn’t sit in a queue. When a job needs a licensed specialist, Michael brings one in but stays accountable for the result, so you still deal with one person.

What if part of the problem is outside your field?

Big problems are usually several smaller ones tangled together, and most of the work is separating them. Some pieces need a license Sypher doesn’t hold: a CPA, an attorney, a regulatory consultant. Michael brings that person in and coordinates the work, so you still have one point of accountability.

What is fractional leadership, and is it right for us?

You get an experienced operator for a set number of days each month, on a retainer, with ownership of agreed KPIs. It fits a company that needs someone senior to run things but can’t yet justify a full-time executive. It fits less well when the job is mostly day-to-day supervision.

What is forensic bookkeeping, and how is it different from an audit?

It means rebuilding the books from bank and card statements, then tracing each exception to its source: a duplicate payment, unbilled work, a cost coded to the wrong job. It is not an audit. Sypher is not a CPA firm, so findings are written up for your CPA or attorney to act on.

Do you work on medical devices and formulations?

Yes, at the planning stage. Michael works with inventors on the problem definition and user needs, and then on prior art and the literature. After that comes a plan for the prototype or formulation work, with sources for materials and lab capacity. He researches the likely regulatory pathway; submissions are made with qualified regulatory counsel.

Can a small business use AI without a large IT budget?

Usually, yes. The audit looks at workflows before tools, for repetitive work done at volume: intake, scheduling, document prep. Much of it can be automated in the Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 you already pay for, with a person reviewing the output.

How are engagements priced?

Defined projects are a fixed fee. Fractional leadership and advisory run on monthly retainers. Every engagement is scoped in writing, with deliverables and milestones agreed before work starts.

How do I see how the work is going?

Through the client portal. You answer Michael’s questions there; he reviews and publishes progress updates as the work moves.

Where do you work?

Sypher is based in the Southwest and works with clients nationwide. Engagements are remote-first, with on-site time where it matters.
