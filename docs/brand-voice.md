# Sypher Solutions: brand voice

How Sypher writes: the website, proposals, reports, emails, posts. The aim is copy that reads like an experienced operator wrote it on purpose. That means specific, plain, a little dry, and confident enough not to oversell.

Run the checker before publishing anything new:

```sh
node scripts/voice-lint.mjs              # whole site + Solutions Index data
node scripts/voice-lint.mjs insights/new-essay.html
```

---

## 1. Who is speaking

- **The principal, to an owner, across a desk.** Write as if Michael is explaining his view to someone who runs a business and has thirty minutes.
- **"I" when it's Michael's view or Michael's work** (About, notes, essays, the portal). **"Sypher" or no subject** for the firm's offer. Use **"we" sparingly.** A one-principal firm that says "our team" fifteen times sounds like it's hiding the size of the firm, and the size is the selling point.
- **Address the reader as "you,"** and assume they're smart and busy.

## 2. The six habits

1. **Name the mechanism.** "Rigorous reconciliation" says nothing. "A three-way match of purchase order, receiving ticket and invoice" tells an owner you've done it before.
2. **Use real numbers, or none.** Ranges are fine ("15 to 25 thousand words"). Invented precision is not. If it isn't verifiable, cut it.
3. **Prefer two items or four, not three.** Lists of three in every paragraph are the strongest stylistic tell there is. Use them when the content really has three parts.
4. **Vary the length.** Mix a long sentence that carries the reasoning with a short one that lands it. Don't make every paragraph three sentences.
5. **Say what you'd say out loud.** If you wouldn't say "leverage synergies" to a client's face, don't write it.
6. **Concede things.** Real experts say when something doesn't fit, what it costs and when to hire someone else. Each concession buys credibility for the next claim.

## 3. Constructions to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Not just X, but Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y" | Formulaic contrast | Say Y. |
| "Whether you're a startup or an enterprise…" | Throat-clearing | Name who it's for. |
| Em dashes used for drama | A strong tell in current AI copy | A full stop, a colon or a comma. |
| Paired slogans ("Be found. Be chosen.") everywhere | Fine once; a pattern by the third | One per page, at most. |
| Rhetorical questions as openers | Filler | Open with the claim or the fact. |
| Intensifiers ("truly", "incredibly") | They weaken the noun | Cut them. |
| Ending sections with a moral ("Because at the end of the day…") | Sermon | End on the fact or the next step. |

**Banned words:** seamless, elevate, unlock, empower, leverage (as a verb), synergy, robust, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, holistic, streamline, journey, delve, tapestry, transformative, revolutionize, supercharge, unparalleled, meticulous, passionate, one-stop, game-changer, "in today's fast-paced world", "rest assured", "peace of mind".

## 4. Working vocabulary by practice

Use these where they're accurate; they show fluency. Don't stack them.

**Launch (feasibility & startup).** Unit economics, contribution margin, break-even volume, ramp, working capital, pro forma, base case and stress case, sensitivity, decision gates, use of funds, capital stack, go / no-go.

**Grow (marketing, SEO, sales ops).** Positioning, ideal customer profile, offer, search intent, Google Business Profile, NAP consistency, schema markup, Core Web Vitals, internal linking, pipeline stages, stage conversion, cadence, CRM hygiene, referral partners.

**Run (projects & operations).** SOPs, decision rights, operating rhythm, weekly scorecard, critical path, change orders, risk register, vendor terms, onboarding plan.

**Modernize (IT, AI, web).** Workflow audit, intake, human review, guardrails, data handling, access control, MFA, stack rationalization, Google Workspace / Microsoft 365, hours returned per week.

**Fund (grants & programs).** NOFO, eligibility screen, SAM.gov registration and UEI, cost share or match, indirect costs, logic model, letters of commitment, reimbursement basis, post-award reporting, On-the-Job Training, Work-Based Learning, SBIR Phase I / II.

**Lead (fractional).** Operating cadence, owner of record, KPIs, board or investor reporting, hiring plan, handover.

**Invent (biomedical device & formulation R&D).** User needs, design inputs and outputs, design controls, design history file, prior art, predicate device, device classification, 510(k) / De Novo, ISO 13485, biocompatibility (ISO 10993), design of experiments, excipients, stability, contract lab, CDMO, tech transfer. *Regulatory submissions are made with qualified regulatory counsel; say so.*

**Recover (forensic bookkeeping & entitlements).** Bank reconciliation, three-way match, AP and AR aging, duplicate payments, unapplied cash, retainage receivable, cost coding, chart of accounts, revenue leakage, R&D credit, Work Opportunity Tax Credit, 8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB, DBE, land-use entitlements, zoning, variance. *Sypher is not a CPA or law firm; findings go to the client's CPA or attorney.*

## 5. Before and after

> **Before:** We deliver seamless, end-to-end solutions that empower businesses to unlock their full potential.
>
> **After:** Most owners don't need another vendor. They need someone who can read the P&L, fix the CRM and write the grant, and who will still be there when the work gets dull.

> **Before:** Our forensic bookkeeping services provide peace of mind by ensuring your financials are accurate.
>
> **After:** We rebuild the books from bank and card statements, tie every account out, and follow each exception to its source. Then we put a dollar figure on it.

> **Before:** Whether you're a startup or an established business, we have the expertise to help you navigate funding.
>
> **After:** Before anyone writes a word of a grant, check three things: SAM.gov registration (it can take weeks), the match requirement, and whether the program reimburses or advances funds.

## 6. Proof

- **Name the work, not the client.** Keep sector and state; drop names unless you have written permission.
- **Show the deliverable.** A list of what was actually handed over beats an adjective.
- **Keep claims to what you can back** with a document, a reference or a number.

## 7. Visual voice (for designers)

- Serif display (Cormorant Garamond) at one weight, with italics for emphasis, used once per heading at most.
- Labels in tracked small capitals; body text in Jost at a comfortable measure (around 60–72 characters).
- Old-style numerals in running text, lining numerals in tables.
- Champagne is an accent for rules, numerals and the occasional key word. It is never a background for body text.
- Generous margins. The page should look like it was set, not filled.
